(1) CLAIMS THE DATA CLEARLY SUPPORTS
  a. Every LLM condition compresses the response distribution. Qwen3-8B question-level variance
     ratio (pred/real): baseline .20, persona .60, axioms .47, persona+axioms .47 (real split-half 1.00).
     Distribution match (1 - EMD/(K-1), split-half): .69 / .83 / .78 / .78 vs real-vs-real ceiling .97.
     All conditions are far below the ceiling and below both reference baselines (group modal .87,
     LR-on-other-answers .93). Stable across the 3 runs (differences <= .01).
  b. Demographic personas collapse respondents WITHIN political groups more than axioms do. Within
     party x ideology groups, the variance ratio is higher for axioms than for the persona in all 5 models:
     Qwen3-8B +.20 [+.13,+.22], 32B-thinking +.25, 32B-instruct +.29, Llama-70B +.11, Distill +.39
     (all q <= .02); distribution match also higher in 4/5 models (+.013 to +.067). Persona+axioms
     sits in between. This is the Bisbee mechanism, in the political dimension where personas
     carry the stereotype.
  c. The persona replicates demographic regression coefficients better than axioms (expected:
     only the persona shows the demographics). Split-half like-for-like comparison: persona − axioms
     slope +.18 to +.24 and correlation +.12 to +.19 in all 5 models (CIs exclude 0 except Distill corr).
  d. Training (curriculum DPO) does not change the response distribution. Trained − untrained on
     the test 100: question-level match −.004 [−.011,+.003], within-group variance ratio +.012
     [−.017,+.041], modal excess −.006 [−.016,+.007]; a second trained run agrees; all differences are
     smaller than the gap between two untrained runs (run1 − run0 match +.010 [+.003,+.020]).

(2) WEAK OR NULL RESULTS
  a. Overall (not within-political-group) the persona matches the real distribution BETTER than axioms
     for 4/5 models: Qwen3-8B question-level match −.049 [−.059,−.042] and variance ratio −.13 for
     axioms vs persona; within non-political groups (gender, religion, region…) persona also preserves
     more variance (.58-.62 vs .46-.49). Only Distill-Llama-70B favours axioms on the group-pooled metrics (and even it is worse on ordinal question-level match). So the
     axioms advantage is specific to within-political-group dispersion, not a general distributional win.
  b. Coefficient inflation under personas. After correcting for noise in the real coefficients
     (reliability ~.33 at n=500), persona slopes are ~1.4-1.8 (inflation, as in Bisbee), axioms
     ~.6-.9, persona+axioms ~1.0-1.4, in all 5 models. The correction's interval reflects only
     reliability uncertainty and assumes classical errors-in-variables. Directional, not a clean estimate.
  c. Modal collapse is small in every LLM condition (excess modal share −.07 to +.06) and much
     smaller than the group-modal baseline (+.20). The models compress variance without
     systematically landing on the REAL group mode. Among wrong answers to deviating respondents
     (ordinal Qs), 44-71% are the group mode vs 37% chance, in every condition incl. no-persona (44-59%);
     where group mode != population mode (~230 items) the persona does not pull toward the group
     mode more than axioms (Qwen3-8B .39 vs .48). Descriptive, no CI.
  d. Analysis 3 is null / reversed. On deviating items, predicting the group mode is NOT associated
     with lower faithfulness once question fixed effects are included: axioms coef ≈ 0 in all 3
     Qwen runs and 32B; persona+axioms (Qwen3-8B, deviating & wrong) is POSITIVE (+.015 to +.023,
     q<.001, all 3 runs; not replicated in 32B). Contradiction rate: null everywhere. The raw gap
     (F .93 if mode vs .98 if not) is entirely question composition.
  e. Analysis 4 regression and Analysis 3 on the test set are uninformative: real-coefficient
     split-half reliability at n=100 is ~.06, so any training contrast on coefficients is noise.

(3) WHAT NOT TO CLAIM
  - Not "axioms preserve the real response distribution better than personas". Overall they do
    not, for 4 of 5 models. The defensible claim is narrower: within political groups.
  - Not "training reduces collapse / improves distributional fidelity". It is null, with a run-to-run
    reference that is as large as the training difference.
  - Not "unfaithful reasoning goes with stereotyped/modal answers". Within question it does not; if
    anything the reverse in one model.
  - Not "LLMs collapse to the group modal answer". They compress variance; their modal share is
    close to real; the group-modal baseline is far more collapsed.
  - Not raw coefficient slopes < 1 as "compression": real coefficients are noisy (split-half r = .20
    at n=250), which attenuates the slope regardless of the model.
  - Not anything about the trained model under persona-only or axioms-only conditions (never generated).
  - Secondary-model names (32B instruct, Llama-70B, Distill-Llama-70B) are unverified.

Interval method note: plug-in EMD/JS/entropy and coefficient correlations are biased under
respondent resampling; distribution metrics use basic bootstrap intervals; regression comparisons use
paired split-halves (a2_condition_contrasts.csv) instead of bootstrap CIs.
